## Introduction

Heart failure (HF) is a chronic and progressive condition associated with significant morbidity, mortality, and healthcare utilization ([McMurray et al., 2019](#mcmurray-2019)). It affects more than 6 million adults in the United States and remains a leading cause of hospitalization, particularly among older adults ([Virani et al., 2021](#virani-2021)). Despite advances in pharmacologic therapy, patients with HF continue to experience recurrent exacerbations, resulting in frequent hospital admissions and decreased quality of life ([Solomon et al., 2022](#solomon-2022)).

Outpatient management plays a critical role in preventing disease progression and reducing hospitalization rates. Guideline-directed medical therapy (GDMT) has evolved to include multiple pharmacologic agents that target different pathophysiological mechanisms ([McMurray et al., 2019](#mcmurray-2019); [Heidenreich et al., 2022](#heidenreich-2022)). Among these, sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 (SGLT2) inhibitors have emerged as a major advancement in HF management ([Zannad et al., 2020](#zannad-2020)).

Dapagliflozin, initially developed for type 2 diabetes, has demonstrated cardiovascular benefits independent of glycemic control, including reductions in heart failure hospitalization and cardiovascular mortality ([Solomon et al., 2022](#solomon-2022); [Packer, 2020](#packer-2020)). These findings have led to its incorporation into contemporary HF management guidelines. However, variability in outcomes across HF populations, particularly between reduced and preserved ejection fraction, highlights the need for further evaluation of its clinical impact in outpatient settings.
