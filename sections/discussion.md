## Discussion: Synthesis of Evidence

Across all studies, dapagliflozin consistently reduced heart failure hospitalizations. This effect was observed across multiple populations and study designs, supporting strong external validity. Importantly, reductions were seen not only in first hospitalizations but also in recurrent events, which are highly relevant in outpatient care ([Jhund et al., 2021](#jhund-2021)).

The evidence for cardiovascular mortality reduction is present but more variable. Patients with reduced ejection fraction appear to derive the greatest benefit, while those with preserved EF show stronger reductions in hospitalization than mortality ([Solomon et al., 2022](#solomon-2022)). These findings are consistent with current heart failure guidelines recommending SGLT2 inhibitors as foundational therapy ([Heidenreich et al., 2022](#heidenreich-2022)).

Methodological strengths include large randomized trials and consistent findings across studies ([McMurray et al., 2019](#mcmurray-2019)). However, common limitations include heterogeneity of populations, reliance on secondary analyses, and combining trials with different follow-up durations and thus the effect of treatment on total events may differ over longer observation periods ([Chatur et al., 2023](#chatur-2023)).

Overall, the literature demonstrates a consistent and clinically meaningful benefit of dapagliflozin in reducing HF-related morbidity.

## Discussion: Implications for Practice, Policy, and Future Research

Dapagliflozin should be considered a core component of GDMT in outpatient HF management due to its consistent reduction in hospitalization and improvement in clinical outcomes ([Zannad et al., 2020](#zannad-2020); [Heidenreich et al., 2022](#heidenreich-2022)). From a policy perspective, improving access to SGLT2 inhibitors is essential to reduce healthcare costs associated with HF hospitalizations.

Future research should focus on long-term outcomes, diversifying patient populations, and analyzing real-world effectiveness of dapagliflozin ([Solomon et al., 2022](#solomon-2022)).
