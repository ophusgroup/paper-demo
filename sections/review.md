## Critical Review of Studies

### Dapagliflozin in Patients with Heart Failure and Reduced Ejection Fraction (McMurray et al., 2019)

Commonly referred to as 'DAPA-HF', this randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial demonstrated that dapagliflozin reduced the composite outcome of worsening HF or cardiovascular death (HR 0.74, 95% CI 0.65–0.85) ([McMurray et al., 2019](#mcmurray-2019)). Strengths include strong internal validity, large sample size, and clinically meaningful endpoints. A limitation is its focus on heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF) populations, versus including populations with other forms of heart failure (however, notable that limiting to HFrEF populations likely allowed researchers to measure clear mortality and morbidity benefits, whereas including other forms of heart failure (such as preserved ejection fraction, (HFpEF) would have introduced heterogeneity that might have made demonstrating drug efficacy challenging) ([Murphy, 2020](#murphy-2020)).

### Dapagliflozin in Heart Failure with Mildly Reduced or Preserved Ejection Fraction (Solomon et al., 2022)

This paper, commonly referred to as 'DELIVER' (Dapagliflozin Evaluation to Improve the Lives of Patients with Preserved Ejection Fraction Heart Failure), reported on a phase 3, international, multicenter, parallel-group, event-driven, double-blind, randomized, controlled trial. A total of 10,418 patients were screened at three-hundred and fifty-three (353) centers in 20 countries; of these patients, 6,263 were randomly assigned to receive dapagliflozin or matching placebo. Following the MacMurray et al ([2019](#mcmurray-2019)) / DAPA-HF paper, this trial paper expanded the evidence to patients with mildly reduced or preserved ejection fraction and demonstrated a reduction in the composite outcome (HR 0.82) ([Solomon et al., 2022](#solomon-2022)). Strengths include broad applicability and strong external validity. However, the effect on cardiovascular mortality alone was less pronounced.

### Effect of Dapagliflozin on Outpatient Worsening of Patients With Heart Failure and Reduced Ejection Fraction: A Prespecified Analysis of DAPA-HF (Docherty et al., 2020)

This prespecified secondary analysis of the DAPA-HF randomized control trial ([Solomon et al., 2019](#mcmurray-2019)) demonstrated that dapagliflozin reduced outpatient worsening HF events by 26%, which was not part of the primary DAPA-HF trial analysis endpoint, but used the same randomized, placebo-controlled, double-blind study design, underscored dapaglifozin's relevance in ambulatory care settings ([Docherty et al., 2020](#docherty-2020)). Limitations included reliance on secondary data, as well as the duration of follow-up was relatively short. As such, related events may differ over time according to HF severity and follow-up. These limitations could affect the generalizability of the researchers' results.

### Dapagliflozin and Recurrent Heart Failure Hospitalizations in Heart Failure With Reduced Ejection Fraction: An Analysis of DAPA-HF (Jhund et al., 2021)

This secondary analysis study of the DAPA-HF trial noted the efficacy of dapagliflozin on reducing the rate of total (i.e. first and repeat) hospitalizations for heart failure and cardiovascular deaths: HF hospitalizations by about 29% (rate ratio 0.71), and reduced cardiovascular death by about 19% (hazard radio (HR) 0.81). In other words, dapagliflozin reduced the combined rate of total HF hospitalizations and cardiovascular death by about 25% (rate ratio 0.75) in patients with HFrEF, highlighting dapagliflozin's impact beyond first events ([Jhund et al., 2021](#jhund-2021)). This is particularly important given the recurrent nature of HF hospitalizations. A limitation, common to clinical trials and secondary analyses, was that the follow-up time was limited, and the effect of treatment on total events might be different over longer observation periods: the researchers were only able to study two (2) types of events (HF hospitalizations; cardiovascular death).

### Effect of Dapagliflozin on Total Heart Failure Events in Patients With Heart Failure With Mildly Reduced or Preserved Ejection Fraction — A Prespecified Analysis of the DELIVER Trial (Jhund et al. 2023)

This prespecified secondary analysis underscored a 23% reduction in total HF events, using advanced statistical modeling to capture recurrent outcomes, and was consistent across a range of subgroups, including across the spectrum of ejection fraction ([Jhund et al., 2023](#jhund-2023)). A limitation includes reliance on secondary analysis, as well as common to clinical trials and secondary analyses: the follow-up time was limited, and the effect of treatment on total events might be different over longer observation periods.

### Effects of dapagliflozin on heart failure hospitalizations according to severity of inpatient course: Insights from DELIVER and DAPA-HF (Chatur et al, 2023)

This pooled (not pre-specified) meta-analysis demonstrated consistent reductions in HF hospitalizations, including length of stays (LOS) across the ejection fraction spectrum. Dapagliflozin significantly reduced total uncomplicated hospitalizations by ~32% (RR 0.68, 95% CI 0.58–0.79) and complicated hospitalizations by ~22% (RR 0.78, 95% CI 0.65–0.94) in HF across the spectrum of ejection fraction (EF). Strength includes improved generalizability. Limitations include, it was not a pre-specified analysis, as well as definitions used to identify complicated and uncomplicated HF hospitalizations were not independently validated nor standardized across regions and institutional practices, and thus may be influenced by respective subjective decision making.

### SGLT2 inhibitors in patients with heart failure with reduced ejection fraction: a meta-analysis of the EMPEROR-Reduced and DAPA-HF trials (Zannad et al. 2020)

This pre-specified meta-analysis of the DAPA-HF (assessing dapagliflozin) and EMPEROR-Reduced (assessing empagliflozin) underscored that these two (2) SGLT2 inhibitors significantly reduce ~23% the rate of cardiovascular death and HF hospitalization in patients with heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF).

Strengths lie in high-level evidence: focus on hard clinical outcomes in HFrEF, use of two (2) large, event-driven RCTs and their data, and clinically meaningful effect sizes. Limitations include combining trials with different follow-up durations (ranging from 60 days to > 4 years), and thus the effect of treatment on total events might be different over longer observation periods.
