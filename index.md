## Abstract

Heart failure (HF) remains a leading cause of hospitalization and cardiovascular mortality despite advances in guideline-directed medical therapy (GDMT). Dapagliflozin, a sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 (SGLT2) inhibitor, has emerged as a key therapy across the heart failure spectrum. The purpose of this paper is to evaluate the effectiveness of dapagliflozin compared with standard therapy on hospitalization rates and cardiovascular mortality in adults with heart failure in the outpatient setting over a 12-month period. A systematic literature search was conducted using PubMed, Cumulative Index to Nursing and Allied Health Literature (CINAHL), and the Cochrane Library for studies published between 2016 and 2026. Seven (7) high-quality studies were selected, including randomized controlled trials (RCTs), secondary data analyses (SDAs), and a meta-analysis. The evidence consistently demonstrates significant reductions in heart failure hospitalizations and improvements in composite cardiovascular outcomes, with hazard ratios ranging from 0.74 to 0.82. Mortality benefits are more pronounced in patients with reduced ejection fractions. Limitations include heterogeneity of populations and reliance on secondary analyses. Overall, dapagliflozin represents a strong evidence-based intervention for improving outcomes in outpatient heart failure management.
